How to Play Chopsticks (Finger Game): Rules, Strategy & Variants

Chopsticks is the most strategic finger game you can play without any equipment. Two players, two hands each, simple counting rules — and a depth that keeps games interesting for years. Here's everything you need to know: setup, turns, splitting, revival, and the strategies that separate beginners from experts.

Setup and starting position

Both players hold out both hands with one finger extended on each hand. No equipment needed. The game is played entirely with fingers.

Decide who goes first (rock-paper-scissors, coin flip, or whoever is younger). Players alternate turns.

How a turn works: attacking

On your turn, you tap one of your hands against one of your opponent's hands. The opponent's tapped hand gains fingers equal to the number of fingers on your attacking hand.

Elimination rule

If a hand reaches 5 or more fingers, that hand is eliminated — it drops to zero (a closed fist). A hand with zero fingers cannot attack or be attacked. If both of a player's hands are eliminated, that player loses.

Splitting: the key strategic move

Instead of attacking, you can use your turn to split fingers between your own two hands. This is the move that adds deep strategy to Chopsticks.

Splitting rules

  • You redistribute fingers between your two hands however you like, as long as the total stays the same
  • The resulting position must be different from your current position (you can't split 2-2 into 2-2)
  • Fractional splits are not allowed (you can't split 3 into 1.5 + 1.5)

Revival: bringing back a dead hand

If you have a dead hand (0 fingers) and your live hand has at least 2 fingers, you can split to bring the dead hand back. For example: 4-0 → 2-2 or 3-0 → 2-1 (with a single finger there is nothing to share). This is called revival and is one of the most important tactics in the game. A player who can revive effectively is much harder to eliminate.

Example: You have 4 on your right hand and 0 (dead) on your left. On your turn, instead of attacking, you split: 4 → 2+2. Now both hands are active again.

3 winning tactics

1. Don't hand your opponent a kill

Before you tap, look at the total you are about to leave on their hand. If it gives them a hand that can eliminate one of yours next turn (see the cheat sheet below), you are making the kill for them.

2. Often worth attacking the hand closer to 5

It is often worth eliminating a hand that is already at 3 or 4 fingers: finishing it off removes one of your opponent's attacking options. Check first that the move does not leave one of your own hands exposed.

3. Split to avoid a fatal attack (split rules only)

If your opponent could attack and eliminate one of your hands next turn, check whether splitting can move your vulnerable hand to a safer count. Proactive splitting is defense — don't only split to revive.

Who wins? Chopsticks is a solved game

Every position has a finite number of moves, so a computer can search the whole game. We did that for the basic tap-only rules used in our online game (both hands start at 1, and a hand that reaches 5 or more is out). The result: the player who moves second can always force a win, in at most 8 turns in total — the game above is one such line. We also checked the classic version with the split described in this guide. If the split may leave one hand at 0 (for example 1 · 1 → 2 · 0) and cannot simply swap your two hands (1 · 2 → 2 · 1), the second player still wins, although some positions are drawn. If the split must always keep both hands above 0, or if swapping hands counts as a different arrangement, the full game is a draw with perfect play. In practice nobody plays perfectly, so the cheat sheet below matters more than the theory.

Use it both ways: before you tap, check whether the table hands you a kill; before you end your turn, check which of your own hands the opponent could eliminate.

Common variants

Modular / Rollover rules

In this variant, instead of elimination at 5, hands "rollover": 5 becomes 0, 6 becomes 1, 7 becomes 2, and so on (modulo 5). This eliminates most instant-kill moves and makes games significantly longer.

Lower-threshold variant (out at 4)

Some versions use 4 as the elimination threshold instead of 5. This makes the game faster, since the maximum attack value is 3. Useful for younger players or quicker games.

No-split variant

Splitting is removed entirely. The game becomes purely offensive — the player who can build higher finger counts faster wins. Much simpler but loses most of the strategic depth.

Why Chopsticks is worth learning

Chopsticks is one of the few games requiring zero equipment that has genuine strategic depth. Unlike Rock Paper Scissors — which is fundamentally random against a skilled player — Chopsticks rewards forward planning, board-state awareness, and opponent modeling. It is a playground game known under different names (Sticks, Finger Chess), with broadly similar rules wherever it is played.

For a game that takes 30 seconds to learn, it has an unusually high ceiling — which is exactly why it travels well across generations and geographies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you play Chopsticks finger game?

Each player starts with one finger up on each hand. On your turn you tap one of your hands against one of your opponent's hands. The tapped hand gains fingers equal to your attacking hand. When a hand reaches 5 or more fingers, it is eliminated (goes to zero). The goal is to eliminate both of the opponent's hands.

Can you play Chopsticks online against the computer?

Yes. Our free Chopsticks game lets you play against an AI at easy, medium or hard difficulty, with a friend on the same device, or watch the CPU play both sides. No sign-up is needed, and it uses the basic tap-only rules.

What is splitting in Chopsticks?

Splitting is a special move where, instead of attacking, you redistribute fingers between your own two hands. For example, if you have 4 fingers on one hand and 0 on the other, you can split to have 2 on each hand. You can only split if the result is different from your current state.

Can you revive a dead hand in Chopsticks?

Yes, when the split rule is in use. This is called 'revival': if your live hand has at least 2 fingers, you can split to bring the dead hand back — for example 4-0 becomes 2-2. Do not confuse it with 'rollover', a different variant in which totals wrap around instead of eliminating a hand. Our online game plays without the split, so revival is not available there.

What happens when a hand reaches exactly 5 in Chopsticks?

In the standard rules, a hand at exactly 5 (or any number ≥ 5) is eliminated and goes to 0. Some variant rules use 'modular arithmetic' where 5 becomes 0, 6 becomes 1, 7 becomes 2, etc. The most common version treats any hand at 5 or above as eliminated.

Who wins at Chopsticks, the first or the second player?

With the basic tap-only rules, a computer search shows that the second player can always force a win within 8 turns in total. Since nobody plays perfectly, spotting eliminations and protecting hands with 4 fingers matters more than the theory.

What is the best opening move in Chopsticks?

Without the split, the first move is forced: with one finger on each hand, the only possible tap is a 1 onto a 1, giving your opponent a 2. Under the tap-only rules that already leaves the first player at a theoretical disadvantage (see “Who wins?” above), although real games are decided by mistakes rather than by the opening. With the split described in this guide, 1 · 1 could also be split into 2 · 0.
